How Much Does It Cost to Relocate to Canada from Nigeria in 2026?

Last reviewed: October 2026

Relocating from Nigeria to Canada can be expensive, but there is no single amount that everyone needs. Your total budget depends on whether you are moving for employment, education, permanent residence or another eligible immigration pathway.

You must consider more than visa fees. Depending on your situation, you may also need money for documents, language tests, medical examinations, flights, accommodation and daily living expenses.

This guide explains the main costs to investigate before making a relocation plan.

Important: Figures in this article are in Canadian dollars (CAD) unless stated otherwise. Government fees and financial requirements can change. Confirm the current amount on the official Canadian government website before submitting an application.

1. What Costs Should You Budget For?

Start by identifying which of these expenses apply to your situation:

  • Immigration application fees.
  • Biometrics fees, if required.
  • Passport and document preparation costs.
  • Language tests or educational credential assessments, if required.
  • Medical examinations and police certificates, where applicable.
  • Flight tickets and baggage.
  • Accommodation after arrival.
  • Food, transportation and telephone expenses.
  • Required proof of funds for your immigration pathway.
  • Emergency savings.

Not every applicant will pay every expense on this list. Your budget should reflect your actual application requirements.

2. Canadian Work Permit Fees

If you intend to work in Canada, first determine whether you qualify for an employer-specific work permit or an eligible open work permit. Having a job offer does not automatically mean you qualify for a work permit.

The standard published government fees include:

Application fee Amount
Standard work permit CAD $155
Biometrics for an individual, if required CAD $85
Additional open work permit holder fee, when applicable CAD $100

For an applicant who must pay the standard work permit fee and individual biometrics fee, those charges total CAD $240. Eligible open work permit applicants may also have to pay the additional CAD $100 fee.

These figures do not include flights, accommodation, document expenses or other personal costs. Fees depend on the type of application.

Check the official instructions here: Apply for a Canadian work permit from outside Canada.

3. Canadian Study Permit Costs

International students need to plan for both immigration expenses and the cost of studying. Tuition can be a substantial expense, and the amount varies by institution and program.

The standard study permit processing fee is CAD $150. Individual biometrics cost CAD $85 when required.

How much money must a student show?

For study permit applications made on or after September 1, 2026, the published minimum living-expense amounts for applicants studying outside Quebec are:

Family size Required annual living expenses
1 person CAD $23,448
2 people CAD $29,192
3 people CAD $35,888
4 people CAD $43,572

These amounts are for living expenses and do not include tuition or transportation. The amount depends on the number of family members included in the application. Quebec has separate requirements.

Students must also demonstrate that they can cover their tuition and transportation expenses. Meeting the financial requirement does not guarantee that a study permit will be approved.

Check the official information before applying:

  • Canadian study permit information
  • Study permit proof-of-funds requirements
Remember: Proof of funds is not a visa fee. It is evidence that you have sufficient money available to meet the applicable financial requirements.

4. Permanent Residence Application Costs

Some Nigerians may qualify for permanent residence through Express Entry, a provincial nominee program or another eligible immigration pathway.

Permanent residence applications may involve processing fees, a right of permanent residence fee, biometrics, language tests, educational credential assessments and supporting documents.

The published federal economic immigration fee schedule lists the following amounts for the principal applicant:

Fee Amount
Processing fee CAD $990
Right of permanent residence fee CAD $600
Combined total for these two fees CAD $1,590

Other fees may apply depending on your application and family members. This is not the complete cost of the immigration process.

Official source: Canadian immigration application fee list.

5. Express Entry Proof of Funds

Some Express Entry applicants must demonstrate that they have enough money to support themselves and their family after arriving in Canada.

The required amount depends on family size and the current rules. Certain applicants, including eligible Canadian Experience Class applicants, may be exempt from the settlement-funds requirement.

The money must meet the government's requirements for accessibility and proof. For example, the government says applicants cannot use money borrowed from another person as settlement funds.

Before relying on a particular amount, check: Official Express Entry proof-of-funds requirements.

Do not confuse proof of funds with money you pay to the government. These are different requirements. A person may need to demonstrate settlement funds in addition to paying application fees.

6. Flight Costs from Nigeria to Canada

Flight prices depend on your departure airport, destination, travel date, airline, baggage allowance and whether your journey is direct or includes connections.

Rather than relying on an old price estimate, compare available fares for your intended travel dates.

Before purchasing a ticket, check:

  • The final price, including taxes and mandatory charges.
  • Checked and cabin baggage allowances.
  • Whether a transit visa is required for any connecting country.
  • Refund and ticket-change conditions.
  • The cost of travelling from the arrival airport to your accommodation.

Avoid making non-refundable travel arrangements before confirming the relevant immigration and travel requirements.

7. Accommodation and Living Expenses

Your first few weeks in Canada may require money for temporary accommodation, rent, food, local transportation and essential household items.

Living expenses vary considerably between cities and communities. Research your intended destination instead of assuming that the cost of living is the same throughout Canada.

Before travelling, investigate:

  • Current rent for a room or apartment.
  • Whether utilities are included in the rent.
  • Public transportation costs.
  • How far your accommodation is from your workplace or school.
  • Whether you have reliable temporary accommodation arranged.
  • How much emergency money you can retain.

If an employer offers accommodation or relocation assistance, confirm the arrangement directly with the genuine employer. Do not assume that a job offer covers your travel or housing costs.

8. How to Calculate Your Relocation Budget

There is no single relocation budget suitable for every Nigerian. The following checklist can help you estimate your own costs.

  1. Choose your pathway. Determine whether you are pursuing employment, education or permanent residence.
  2. Confirm eligibility. Read the official requirements before paying anyone to assist you.
  3. Calculate application fees. Use the current government fee schedule for your application.
  4. Estimate document expenses. Include any required tests, assessments, medical examinations and certificates.
  5. Research flights. Compare prices for your intended travel dates.
  6. Estimate settlement expenses. Research accommodation, food and transportation in your destination.
  7. Check proof of funds. Confirm whether your pathway requires it and how much applies to your family.
  8. Keep an emergency reserve. Allow for unexpected expenses and delays.

Convert your estimate into naira using a current exchange rate when you are ready to budget. Exchange rates change, so an old naira estimate may quickly become inaccurate.

9. How to Avoid Canada Immigration Scams

People planning to relocate can be targeted by fake employers, fraudulent recruitment agents and individuals who promise guaranteed visas.

  • Verify job offers independently before sending money or personal documents.
  • Be suspicious of anyone promising guaranteed immigration approval.
  • Check government fees through official Canadian government websites.
  • Research immigration representatives and confirm their authorization where required.
  • Do not assume that a company logo or official-looking letter makes a job offer genuine.
  • Read the employment terms carefully and confirm any relocation benefits directly with the employer.

10. Frequently Asked Questions

Can I relocate to Canada without paying an immigration agent?

Some applicants can prepare and submit their own applications. Whether you need professional assistance depends on your circumstances. If you choose to hire a representative, verify their credentials and understand their fees before proceeding.

Does a Canadian job offer mean my employer will pay for everything?

No. Employers do not automatically pay for flights, accommodation or immigration expenses. Confirm any relocation support directly with the genuine employer and review the terms carefully.

Can I use borrowed money as Express Entry proof of funds?

The Canadian government states that settlement funds cannot be borrowed from another person. Check the official requirements to understand what financial evidence is acceptable for your application.

Will paying the application fee guarantee approval?

No. Paying a fee does not guarantee a visa, work permit, study permit or permanent residence application will be approved. Applicants must meet the relevant eligibility and admissibility requirements.

Where can I check the latest immigration fees?

Use the official Canadian immigration fee list and the instructions for your specific program. Third-party websites may contain outdated information.
